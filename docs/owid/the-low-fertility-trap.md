# The Low Fertility Trap

When a country's TFR drops significantly it very rarely recovers. The chart below plots each country's TFR from the year it first drops below a chosen threshold. The majority of countries TFR do not recover over the long term. 

Some countries did see some recovery in TFR:

- Several post-Soviet states, such as Russia, Belarus, and Latvia, fell to lows around 1.1–1.2 after the Soviet collapse before rising to 1.7–1.8 in the mid-2010s: Belarus from 1.19 (2003) to 1.73 (2015), Latvia from 1.10 (1998) to 1.74 (2016), Russia from 1.18 (1999) to 1.79 (2015). All three have since fallen. Czechia saw the largest recovery of any post-Soviet state, from a low of 1.14 in 1999 to 1.82 in 2021, then dropped to 1.45 two years later. 

- Denmark saw a large recovery in TFR. Its TFR fell to 1.38 in 1983 before rising to 1.88 in 2008, and has since fallen to 1.51 in 2023. Sweden, Finland, Belgium and the Netherlands followed simiar trends: each fell to around 1.4–1.5 in the 1970s or 80s, recovered to 1.8–2.0 by around 2010, before falling again.

Although these countries saw some recovery from their lowest TFRs, they were sill well below replacement level for many years, meaning the next generation, and all subsequent generations, will be smaller than the present generations.
