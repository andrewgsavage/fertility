:::{tab-item} Notes
```{note}
Changes: found by the same search for 48 countries; sources and the rule for 'major' are on [Country timelines](country-timelines.md). Rates: ASFR at 20–24 and 25–29 and first-birth rates from Eurostat [demo_frate](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/demo_frate/default/table) and [demo_fordagec](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/demo_fordagec/default/table), the [Human Fertility Database](https://www.humanfertility.org), the [Human Fertility Collection](https://www.fertilitydata.org/) and Uruguay's MSP, 2005–2024. Each line is the % difference from the year before the change, minus the same difference averaged over all other countries in those years. Change in trend: the slope over the year before to 4 years after, minus the slope over 4 years before to the year before, in points a year; a change needs 3 or more years on each side. Changes in one country a year or two apart share years, so they are not independent. Governments often act after births have fallen, so a later rise can be a rebound rather than an effect.
```
:::

::::

Childcare expansions were followed by no change at 20–24 and a small improvement in first births at 25–29, 1.3 points a year on average across 31 expansions, with 60% followed by any improvement; that is less than twice its standard error. Only three cuts met the rule (Finland 2016, the Netherlands 2012 and Slovenia 2012), and all three were followed by a worse trend at 20–24.
