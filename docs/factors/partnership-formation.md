| Poland | 24.1 | 27.9 | 24.5 | 27.9 | 28.7% |

Between 2000 and 2020 the age at first marriage rose by more than the age at first birth in every country with both series: France +5.1 years against +1.1, Spain +6.8 against +2.1, Sweden +4.4 against +1.8, Poland +3.8 against +3.4. Marriage is now later than the first birth in most of these countries, and half or more of births in France, Spain and Sweden are outside marriage. So later marriage is not by itself later childbearing; Poland, where marriage and first birth still coincide, is the closest to the proposed mechanism. Marriage is not the same as partnership, and these are period means, not cohort ages.

Coverage: Eurostat [marriages and divorces](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/demo_nind/default/table) and [fertility indicators](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/demo_find/default/table). Series start in 1990 or later and have gaps (Germany's marriage ages end in 2017; first-birth ages for several countries start after 2000).

## Living in a couple

People aged 25–49 living in a couple household (married or cohabiting, with or without children) as a share of all people in that age group, 2006 onward. Couples who live apart are not counted. Denmark and Sweden are hidden by default: their series change by 15–25 points between years, which looks like a break rather than a trend.

The EU27 share is flat between 2010 and 2024 and moves in both directions across countries: down in France, Finland, the Netherlands, Spain and Italy, up in Czechia and Poland. Fertility fell in all of them. At ages 25–49 there is no common fall in couple formation that would account for the fall in births. The age band is wide: it can't show whether couples form later within the 20s and early 30s, which is where the mechanism acts. Eurostat's [household composition table](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/lfst_hhindws/default/table) (EU Labour Force Survey) has no 25–34 group.
