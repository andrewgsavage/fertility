## First and later pregnancies by age

These charts compare abortions with births of the same order. Abortions to women with no children are set against first births, abortions to women with one child against second births, and abortions to mothers against second and later births. A value of 20 means that for every 100 first pregnancies carried to a birth, 20 others ended in abortion. Unlike the share of abortions to mothers [further down](#abortions-to-women-who-already-have-a-child), the ratio doesn't move just because fewer women are mothers.

### What these show

**Where the first-pregnancy ratio held or fell, women who got pregnant with a first child still kept it.** The fall in first births there is a fall in first pregnancies. At 25–29, the age at which most first births used to happen, the ratio was flat from 2013 to 2024 in Czechia (8 per 100 first births), Slovenia (9) and Italy (13 to 15), and halved in Hungary (13 to 6) and Slovakia (10 to 5). First births at 25–29 fell 30–37% in Czechia, Slovenia and Italy, and first pregnancies (abortions plus first births) fell by almost the same: 33%, 30% and 36%. Once conceived, a first child is kept as often as before. What changed is how many women conceive one. That can be partnership, contraception or postponement, and these charts can't separate them.

**Where the ratio rose, fewer pregnancies still explain most of the fall.** It rose at 20–29 in Spain, Portugal, Finland and Estonia. Spain moved fastest: at 20–24 from 68 to 110 (about +4 a year) and at 25–29 from 22 to 41 (+1.7 a year). Even there, two-thirds of the fall in first births at 25–29 is fewer pregnancies: first births fell 36% and first pregnancies 26%. In Finland at 20–24, first births fell 51% and first pregnancies 44%, so about 80% of the fall is fewer pregnancies. In Portugal at 20–24 it is about half (births −39%, pregnancies −23%, 2008–2024).

**At 30–34 almost every first pregnancy is kept.** The ratio is 4–9 per 100 first births in every country but Spain (14) and Estonia (13), and it barely moved. Women who conceive a first child at the ages most now do mostly carry it to term.

**Since 2020 the ratio has been rising at young ages almost everywhere.** At 20–24 it rose from 2020 to 2024 in every country with data except Hungary: Czechia 17 to 23, Estonia 38 to 50, Finland 44 to 58, Italy 42 to 55, Portugal 36 to 56, Slovakia 10 to 13, Slovenia 22 to 27 and Spain 73 to 110. At 25–29 it rose by 2–6 points in most of them and by 15 in Spain. Up to 2019, first pregnancies were kept as often as before in most of Europe. Since 2020 more are being ended, so far mostly at ages where first births are now uncommon.

**Later pregnancies show family limitation falling in Central Europe and rising in Britain.** For mothers the ratio is highest at the youngest ages and again at 35+, where women stop after the children they want. At 35+ it halved in Hungary (59 to 27, 2013–2023) and Slovakia (46 to 23), and fell in Czechia (43 to 31) and Estonia (64 to 41). It rose at every age in England & Wales (23 to 40, all ages, 2013–2022), and at 25–34 in Finland and Portugal.

These ratios are also rows in the [country comparison](country-comparison.md), against the change in the share of women with a child by 30.

### Abrupt changes and policy

Most of these series move smoothly. The steps below are larger than the usual year-to-year noise. Only some line up with a change in the law or in how abortion is provided.

Abortions and births are rounded to the nearest 100.

Two patterns stand out. In Western and Northern Europe the ratio falls steeply with age: teenagers mostly have more abortions than births, while women aged 30–34 have 7–29 abortions per 100 births. In the former Soviet Union and the Caucasus (Russia, Belarus, Georgia, Azerbaijan, Bulgaria, Romania) the ratio instead rises with age, which fits abortion being used to stop childbearing after the wanted number of children, rather than to postpone the first.

Teenage births are now so rare in Sweden and Norway that teenagers there have about five abortions for every birth. Japan follows the same pattern: 2.5 abortions per teenage birth, but under 9 per 100 births at 30–34. The United States is flatter. Its teenage ratio (38 per 100) is a fifth of Western Europe's, because US teen births are still common: 114,000 in the 43 reporting areas in 2022, against 4,300 in Japan.

## Sources and coverage

- Abortions: Eurostat [demo_fabort](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/demo_fabort/default/table) (legally induced abortions by age of woman), replaced by national statistics where they exist:
  - England & Wales: DHSC [abortion statistics](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/abortion-statistics-in-england-and-wales), residents, 2004–2023 (2014 tables for 2004–2012, 2023 tables for 2013–2023). DHSC has not yet published 2024.
  - Scotland: Public Health Scotland [terminations by health board and age](https://www.opendata.nhs.scot/dataset/termination-of-pregnancy-in-scotland), Scottish residents, 2000–2025.
  - Sweden: Socialstyrelsen [Statistik om aborter 2025](https://www.socialstyrelsen.se/publikationer/statistik-om-aborter-2025-2026-6-10342/), Table 1, 1983–2025. There is no data for 2013, and a new collection method from 2014 means later years aren't fully comparable with earlier ones.
  - Norway: FHI Abortregisteret [statistics bank](https://www.fhi.no/op/abortregisteret/statistikk-fra-abortregisteret/), table A2, 1979–2025.
  - Germany: Destatis [abortions by age group](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Themen/Gesellschaft-Umwelt/Gesundheit/Schwangerschaftsabbrueche/_inhalt.html), 2012–2025 (Eurostat before 2012).
  - Netherlands: IGJ annual Wafz reports ([2021](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/blg-1050878.pdf), [2024](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/blg-1220761.pdf)), 2020–2024. IGJ counts every abortion done in the Netherlands, about a tenth of them for women living abroad. Each year's age groups are scaled to the number for women living in the Netherlands, which assumes both groups have the same age mix.
- United States: CDC [Abortion Surveillance — United States, 2022](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/ss/ss7307a1.htm), Table 4, covering the 43 areas that reported every year from 2013 to 2022. California, DC, Florida, Maine, Maryland, New Hampshire, New Jersey, Tennessee and Wyoming are missing, so the counts cover about three-quarters of US births. CDC gives each age group's share of abortions and its abortion ratio; births in those areas are derived from the two, so they carry the rounding of the ratio (under 1%). 2022 is the latest year CDC has published.
- Japan: MHLW [Report on Public Health Administration and Services](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/toukei/saikin/hw/eisei_houkoku/24/dl/kekka5.pdf) (衛生行政報告例), abortions by age for fiscal years (April–March) 2003–2024, set against HFD births for the calendar year.
- Births: Eurostat [demo_fasec](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/databrowser/view/demo_fasec/default/table) (live births by mother's age, 2007–2024), with the [Human Fertility Database](https://www.humanfertility.org/) for earlier years and for Japan. England & Wales uses [ONS birth registrations](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/birthsdeathsandmarriages/livebirths/datasets/birthsinenglandandwalesbirthregistrations) and Scotland the [NRS births time series](https://nrscotland.gov.uk/publications/births-time-series-data-2024). Where abortions run to 2025 but births stop at 2024, 2025 is left out.
- Age groups are Under 20, 20–24, 25–29, 30–34 and 35+, because DHSC publishes 35 and over as one group. Abortions or births of unknown age are spread across the known groups in proportion. Country-years missing an age group, or with 10% or more of abortions of unknown age, are left out (this drops Armenia, Moldova, Montenegro and Ukraine).
- Countries report abortions to their own residents differently, and abortions done abroad or with pills obtained outside the health system aren't counted. Poland's near-zero figures reflect its law, not an absence of abortion; Albania's figures look too low to be complete.
- Not covered: Denmark after 2014 (its register has moved to an interactive databank with no download), Belgium after 2011, France 2008–2013 (missing from Eurostat; France's own open data starts in 2016), Ireland, Austria, Greece after 2003 and Northern Ireland. Canada publishes abortions by age, but in groups (17 and under, 18–24) that don't match the ones here.
