Pro-natal payments rarely line up with a turn in young fertility; averaged over every change, see [Policy and the state](policy-and-the-state.md). Poland's 500+, paid for second and later children from 2016, is the clearest match: births at 25–29 rose 4% a year from 2015 to 2018, while first births at 25–29 stayed flat. Once 500+ covered first children in 2019, first births at 20–24 fell 10% a year from 2020. Hungary's rates started rising in 2011, five years before its housing grant (CSOK) was enlarged in 2016, and first births rose nearly as fast as all births.

The new or larger payments of 2019–2020 were followed by the steepest falls on the page: Czechia's rates fell about 10% a year from 2020–2021 after its parental allowance went up, and Romania's 25–29 rate fell 10% a year from 2021 after rising 3% a year since 2013. Japan's 20–24 rate fell 10% a year from 2019, the year preschool became free.

Spain's €2,500 birth payment ran from 2007 to 2010; its 20–24 rate rose 7% a year to 2008 and fell 6% a year from then, three years before the payment ended. Finland's 2016 limit on daycare and its 2020 reversal both fall inside unbroken declines; its first-birth decline slowed from 2018, two years before the reversal. In most of Western Europe, the US and Canada, the first break falls in 2008–2010, next to the 2008 crash.

First births and all births mostly turn in the same years and at similar speeds. The exception is Poland at 25–29, where 500+ paid for second and later children.
