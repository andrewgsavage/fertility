Expansions of cash support were followed by a small improvement in young fertility relative to other countries: the trend at 20–24 improved by 1.2 points a year on average across 72 expansions, and by 0.8 at 25–29, with 60% of changes followed by any improvement. Most of that comes from South Korea, whose four payments of 2022–2024 overlap and run into its 2024 rise in births; without them the average is about +0.5 points a year at every age, inside the noise.

Cuts were followed by a slightly worse trend for all births, −0.3 to −0.6 points a year across 23 cuts, and no change in first births; both are too small to tell from no change. The steepest relative falls at 25–29 after an expansion followed Belarus's 2015 family capital, Poland's 2019 extension of 500+ to every child and Latvia's 2022 benefit reform.
