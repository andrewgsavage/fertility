More years in education push first births later. Graduates have tended to have their first child after 30, so a rising share of graduates delays births across the population, and may reduce them if postponed births are not fully made up later.

The UK pages look at this in detail:

- [Education vs Fertility](../uk/education-vs-fertility.md): rising school-leaving ages and university entry for the 1968-75 cohorts opened a childlessness gap at 25-30 that had closed by 40; recent cohorts show a larger fall in early births than education alone explains.
- [Bye Bye Baby?](../uk/childlessness-by-education.md): first-birth rates fell by a similar amount for graduates and non-graduates since 2011, despite the sharper rise in non-graduate childlessness.
