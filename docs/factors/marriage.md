::::

Between 2000 and 2020 the age at first marriage rose by more than the age at first birth in every country with both series: France +5.1 years against +1.1, Spain +6.8 against +2.1, Sweden +4.4 against +1.8, Poland +3.8 against +3.4. Marriage is now later than the first birth in most of these countries, and half or more of births in France, Spain and Sweden are outside marriage. So later marriage is not by itself later childbearing; Poland, where marriage and first birth still coincide, is the closest to the proposed mechanism. Marriage is not the same as partnership, and these are period means, not cohort ages.

## Marriage decline in East Asia

| Study | Data | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Yoo 2026, *Tracking the decline of first marriage in South Korea*, Demographic Research 54(3): 71–86, [10.4054/DemRes.2026.54.3](https://doi.org/10.4054/DemRes.2026.54.3) | Korean vital statistics 1993–2023, tempo-adjusted proportion ever married | First marriage fell for both sexes, and not only through later marriage; men's levels are lower and more sensitive to timing shifts. The 2020 dip was mostly fewer international marriages. |
| Retherford, Ogawa & Matsukura 2001, *Late marriage and less marriage in Japan*, Population and Development Review 27(1): 65–102, [10.1111/j.1728-4457.2001.00065.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1728-4457.2001.00065.x) | Japan 1975–1995 | Singulate mean age at marriage rose from 24.5 to 27.7 (women) and 27.6 to 30.7 (men); projected never-married rose from about 5% to 15% (women) and 6% to 22% (men). |
| Raymo, Park, Xie & Yeung 2015, *Marriage and family in East Asia*, Annual Review of Sociology 41: 471–492, [10.1146/annurev-soc-073014-112428](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-soc-073014-112428) | Later and less marriage and childbearing in China, Japan, Korea and Taiwan, with little change in family expectations. |
