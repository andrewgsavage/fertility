The share fell after 2010 in all 41 countries with a 2010 value. In eight it fell by less than 2.5 points: Germany, the Netherlands, Poland, Algeria, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Cambodia and Kenya. The FT also splits the change in the US TFR into fewer couples and fewer births per couple (American Community Survey, following [Lyman Stone](https://ifstudies.org/blog/no-ring-no-baby)): 1960–1980, −14.0% from fewer couples and −35.7% from fewer births among couples; 2000–2022, −14.5% from fewer couples and −2.5% from fewer births among couples.

## Coupling by education, UK and US

Share of people aged 25–34 in a couple, by whether they have a degree, from the same FT article.

```{raw} html
<iframe src="../_static/ft/ft_coupling_education.html" style="width: 100%; height: 440px; display: block; border: 1px solid var(--color-background-border);" loading="lazy"></iframe>
```

In the US the share without a degree fell from 70.4% (1980) to 48.9% (2024), and with a degree from 66.8% to 57.6%. In the UK the share without a degree fell from 71.1% (1992) to 56.4% (2019), against 66.3% to 61.2% for graduates. The Resolution Foundation's [Bye bye baby](https://www.resolutionfoundation.org/publications/bye-bye-baby/) (Figure 7, ages 25–29, UK) has the same crossover: non-graduates 60% partnered in 1998-99, flat until 2010-11, 53% in 2023-24; graduates 54% to 63% (2010-11) to 57%.

```{note}
FT analysis of the UK Household Longitudinal Study, UK Family Resources Survey and US Current Population Survey. RF analysis of DWP Households Below Average Income.
```
