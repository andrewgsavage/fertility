## Proposed mechanism

## Ideal and actual children in Europe

Figure 2 of the Resolution Foundation's [Bye bye baby](https://www.resolutionfoundation.org/publications/bye-bye-baby/) report compares the average actual and ideal number of children of women aged 40–54 in 2011 and 2020/23. It finds the gap between them widened in seven of eight countries, most in the UK (0.3 to 0.7), driven mainly by fewer actual births rather than lower ideals.

The fall in actual births in Figure 2 doesn't appear in population data. In the Human Fertility Database (HFD), the mean number of children of women aged 40–54 hardly changed between 2011 and 2022: 1.91 to 1.90 in the UK and 1.57 to 1.54 in Germany. The survey figures put UK women at 2.10 in 2011 and 1.66 in 2020/23.

Measured against HFD's actual births, the gap narrowed in five of the eight countries, the UK only slightly (0.45 to 0.42), and widened in three: Czech Republic (0.17 to 0.53), Estonia (0.65 to 0.70) and France (0.46 to 0.48). Ideal family size fell in six countries, rose in the Czech Republic and was flat in France, and stayed above 2.1 in all eight.

## Ideal and actual children in the US

In the General Social Survey, the ideal number of children given by US women aged 40–54 fell from 2.9 in 1972–76 to 2.3 in 1993–96, then rose to 2.4–2.6. Their actual number of children fell from 3.4 to about 2.1 over the same period and has been between 2.1 and 2.4 since. Before the 1990s these women had more children than their stated ideal; since then they have had fewer, by 0.1–0.3 in the survey and 0.1–0.5 in HFD.

## Young adults' ideals by birth cohort

### United States

No US cohort born since 1975 gives a consistently lower ideal number of children than earlier cohorts at the same age. At 18–24, women born 1985–89 gave 2.75 and those born 1990–94 2.96, against 2.56–2.63 for women born 1965–79. Women born 1995–99 gave 2.47 at 18–24, and those born 2000–06 gave 2.77. At 30–34, every cohort from 1965–69 to 1990–94 gave between 2.36 and 2.61.

### Europe

Between 2011 and 2020–23, young women's personal ideal fell in Finland (2.45 to 2.08), the Netherlands (2.49 to 2.21) and Denmark (2.47 to 2.28). It rose in Germany (2.08 to 2.38) and Austria (1.96 to 2.16), and changed by less than 0.05 in the Czech Republic, Estonia and the UK. In 2020–23, women aged 18–29 intended to have 0.13–0.40 fewer children than their ideal, and fewer than two in Finland (1.82), the Czech Republic (1.94), Austria (1.97) and Germany (1.98).

### Finland

Finland has the longest series and a cohort study. In the Family Barometer, the ideal number of children of 20–45-year-olds fell from 2.45 in 2007 to 1.75 in 2018, then rose to 2.05 in 2021 and 2.0 in 2022. In 2018, 20–29-year-olds gave 1.84 and childless 20–29-year-olds 1.70. Pooling barometers from 2007 to 2018, women gave a mean ideal of 2.4 at age 30 if born 1970–74, 2.2 if born 1975–79 and 1.9 if born 1980–89. The share of 25-year-olds whose ideal is no children rose from 2% of women and 4% of men born 1975–79 to 21% and 26% of those born 1990–94.

## Ideal and actual children in DHS countries

In 74 of the 86 countries with a Demographic and Health Survey (DHS) giving both measures, women aged 45–49 have had more children than the average ideal, by up to 3.4 (Mexico 1987: ideal 3.0, actual 6.4). The ideal is above actual births in 12 countries, among them Niger, Chad, Guinea and the Central African Republic, where both are above 5, and Armenia and Ukraine, where both are below 3.
