# Literature Review

Academic papers relevant to this site, with a one-line summary of each paper's finding.

| Paper | Finding |
|---|---|
| Wilkoszewski, H. (2009), [*Age Trajectories of Social Policy Preferences: Support for Intergenerational Transfers from a Demographic Perspective*](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1451738), paper prepared for the APSA Annual Meeting, Toronto. | Older respondents are less supportive of transfers to families but more supportive of pension reforms that shift costs onto younger generations; these age effects are non-linear across the life course and aren't fully explained by life-cycle economic theory alone. |
| Goerres, A., [*Reforming the Welfare State in Times of Grey Majorities: The Myth of an Opposition between Younger and Older Voters in Germany*](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1077604), published in *German Policy Studies*. | Finds little evidence of electoral antagonism between young and old in Germany: differences in party support across age groups stem from generational (cohort) effects tied to early political socialization rather than self-interested life-cycle voting, so age does not function as a political cleavage. |
